1About

I am a veterinarian (DVM) and MS student in Prof. Dong-hun Lee's Wildlife Health Laboratory at the College of Veterinary Medicine, Konkuk University, Seoul. As an undergraduate at Gyeongsang National University, I joined the Laboratory of Veterinary Infectious Diseases, conducting regional surveillance of viral pathogens in wild birds, wild boars and cattle, as well as antimicrobial resistance in poultry.

My master's research focuses on the circulation and genetic diversity of avian paramyxoviruses in reservoir populations. Using whole-genome sequencing and phylogeographic analysis, I trace their transmission within the East Asian–Australasian Flyway and between continents. In parallel, I take part in capacity-building projects at the national public health laboratories of Lao PDR and Tanzania. At both laboratories, I help staff design country-specific primer schemes for dengue virus sequencing and analyze the phylodynamics of priority pathogens, such as measles virus in Lao PDR and Marburg virus in Tanzania.

2Ongoing Research

Viral genomic surveillance in Lao PDR and Tanzania

This project started with capacity-building workshops at the national public health laboratories of Lao PDR and Tanzania. For the workshops, I adapted the analysis pipeline to the primer schemes used in each country and to the computers available at each laboratory. I am now working with both laboratories on phylogeographic analysis of dengue virus sequences from the two countries, and of chikungunya and measles virus sequences from Lao PDR, to find out how these viruses move across borders and which lineages are circulating.

Intercontinental spread of avian paramyxoviruses

I investigated avian paramyxoviruses in AIV-negative wild bird samples using whole-genome sequencing. To understand how these viruses moved between continents and whether East Asia played a role in their spread, I now work on phylogeographic analysis of APMV-1 and APMV-4. Along the way, I also discovered a virulent Newcastle disease virus in a great cormorant and avian orthoavulavirus 16 in a cinereous vulture, a host in which it had not been reported before.

Avian influenza surveillance in wild birds

Participating in the national highly pathogenic avian influenza (HPAI) surveillance program, I collect fecal samples from wild birds across Korea and sequence whole genomes of the viruses detected.
